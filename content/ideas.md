# ideas for [[Dhesyth]]
As the [[Lungroot]] was harvested from the groves, villages and towns came to be in places which was normally not achievable, however, the people of Dhesyth would soon be hit with something awful. A disease known as [[Ruplexia]], emerged out of nowhere and killed thousands of inhabitants.  The disease seemed to spread like a curse, with seemingly no visible way of transference. By chance however, a desperate Druid found out, that if someone came in contact with Dhesyth's poisonous air, the symtoms of Ruplexia seemed to retract.

# Ideas for the Fields of descecration
### **The Betrayal**

King Arvandel was exiled to Prenhul after being framed by his right hand — a man who wanted the throne but lacked the legitimacy to seize it openly. The court believed the valley would kill the king quietly.

It did kill him.

But death did not end him.

### **The Fracturing**

Arvandel died with:

- rage at betrayal
    
- grief for his lost kingdom
    
- guilt for the mistakes he _did_ make
    
- fear of being forgotten
    
- and a desperate, burning desire for justice
    

Those emotions tore his soul apart at the moment of death.

Instead of rising as a single revenant, the king’s soul **fractured into multiple entities**, each one embodying a different facet of who he was — or who he could have been.

These fragments anchored themselves to corpses, bones, or even the land itself.

### **The Result**

Prenhul became home to **several “kings,”** each ruling a different region of the valley:

- **The Wrathful King** — commands the most aggressive undead; wants open war
    
- **The Grieving King** — mourns endlessly; his domain is quiet, fog‑choked, and full of weeping spirits
    
- **The Just King** — believes he must “cleanse” the living world of corruption
    
- **The Hollow King** — a near-mindless echo, wandering the wastes
    
- **The Crownless King** — the most cunning fragment, the one who remembers the betrayal most clearly
    

And one of them — your choice which — has become the **dominant ruler**, the closest thing to the “true” Arvandel.

### **Why the Valley Changed**

Each fragment radiates necromantic energy tied to its emotion. Their overlapping domains warp the land:

- Wrathful lands are scorched and cracked
    
- Grieving lands are cold, misty, and silent
    
- Just lands are orderly, patrolled by disciplined undead
    
- Hollow lands are barren and empty
    
- Crownless lands are full of illusions, traps, and whispers
    

The valley is a patchwork of necromantic biomes.

### **How the Truth Was Revealed**

The first clue came when border scouts encountered an undead rider wearing the king’s armor — but speaking with a voice that was _not_ his.

Then another rider appeared days later, claiming to be Arvandel as well — but with a completely different personality.

When a third “king” was sighted, the truth became undeniable: **the king had returned, but not as one being.**

### **How Prenhul Became the Fields of Desecration**

A priest who survived an encounter with the Wrathful King wrote:

> “The land is desecrated by a king torn into pieces.”

The phrase spread. People stopped calling it Prenhul. It became known as **the Fields of Desecration** — a land ruled by the broken remnants of a dead king.
